* **Hypothesis names a specific mix** — "I expect the optimal mix to be 20 tomato beds, 30 mesclun, and 14 carrots" — three counts, exactly 64, each inside its cap, and repeated in your front matter. Complete.
* **Economic mechanism** — This is where the most points went. The whole justification is one sentence: "because the crops should be planted to maximize profit per bed and I expect them to meet labor availability and fixed cost demands despite labor requirements increasing as more beds of one crop are planted." You acknowledge diminishing returns, but no rate is quoted, no price per bed, and nothing in the argument picks these three numbers rather than any others. The clearest place to see it is carrots: 14 is six beds below the cap on the crop with the lowest labor and the lowest cost, and the brief doesn't say why you would stop there.
* **Falsifiability and process** — "I will know I am wrong if we cannot afford the mix of crops I have listed in either fixed costs or labor costs." That half is concrete and testable. The second half — "if there is a more profitable scenario than the one I've described" — will almost always come true, because some other mix is more profitable unless your guess is exactly right, so it sets no threshold and names no model output. Your process is strong: prompt-log.md states the protocol correctly — "without rewriting it or suggesting replacement wording" — and closes "I did not incorporate the AI critique or use AI-generated wording in my submitted brief." You also labeled your first two entries as reconstructed rather than passing them off as written at the time. That was the right call, and I noticed it.

**A FIXED COST IS NOT A BUDGET**

Your problem statement treats the $20,000 as money you have to spend across the season, and your falsification tests whether you can "afford" the mix. In this case there is no budget at all. The $20,000 is a fixed cost: you owe it whether you plant 64 beds or none, so it can't change which mix is best — it only changes whether the best mix makes a profit.

What limits you is land — 64 beds, and the three caps — and labor — your 720 hours, then temporary workers at 1,440 hours each. Get that right in the brief and two things follow: your mechanism has the right constraints to argue with, and your falsification can test something the model will actually report.
