# Perfect Competition — engagement brief

## The problem

My farm is deciding the optimal mix of 3 different crops that will bring in the most money according to the limits of farming structure. I cannot change that I have 64 beds, a 36- week season, $20,000 as a fixed cost (not an available budget) and 720 of my own hours and each of my 4 temporary workers can provide 1,440 hours. I have to decide how many beds of each crop to plant. The goal is to maximize profits. If I make the wrong decision we could not make enough money to cover our costs and potentially end up losing money or worse, having to close the farm. The case supports the risk of earning insufficient profit but does not establish that one poor allocation would necessarily close the farm.

## What I am assuming

I am assuming market prices will remain fixed. The season will last 36-weeks, no more, no less. All planted output will be sold. The labor formula accurately represents diminishing returns. Temporary labor will be available when we need it. Fertilizer and fixed costs will remain as standing. The farm pays $20,000 regardless of crop mix. The fixed cost affects total profit but does not limit how many beds can be planted.

## Hypothesis

I expect the optimal mix to be 20 tomato beds, 30 mesclun, and 14 carrots because the crops should be planted. Tomatoes earn the highest of the three crops per bed and even though they have the highest diminishing returns rate, I expect their revenue advantage to initially outweigh the labor disadvantage. I expect that similarly, because mesclun earns more than carrots, and has the lowest diminishing return of the three so I expect it to return a higher profit than the carrots. Out of the 64 beds we have, if 20 are allocated to tomatoes and 30 are allocated to mesclun because of their caps, that leaves 14 beds for carrots. 

## How I would know I was wrong

I would know I was wrong if the proposed mix requires more than 6,480 labor hours, violates the 64 bed limit, or if I identify a more profitable feasible allocation.
