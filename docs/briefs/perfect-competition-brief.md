# Perfect Competition — engagement brief

## The problem

My farm is deciding the optimal mix of 3 different crops that will bring in the most money according to the limits of farming structure. I cannot change that I have 64 beds, a 36- week season, $20,000 for the season, and 720 of my own hours and 1,440 of my 4 temporary workers' hours. I have to decide how many beds of each crop to plant. The goal is to maximize profits. If I make the wrong decision we could not make enough money to cover our costs and potentially end up losing money or worse, having to close the farm. Stakes are high.

## What I am assuming

I am assuming market prices will remain fixed. The season will last 36-weeks, no more, no less. All planted output will be sold. The labor formula accurately represents diminishing returns. Temporary labor will be available when we need it. Fertilizer and fixed costs will remain as standing. 

## Hypothesis

I expect the optimal mix to be 20 tomato beds, 30 mesclun, and 14 carrots because the crops should be planted to maximize profit per bed and I expect them to meet labor availability and fixed cost demands despite labor requirements increasing as more beds of one crop are planted. 

## How I would know I was wrong

I will know I am wrong if we cannot afford the mix of crops I have listed in either fixed costs or labor costs. Or if there is a more profitable scenario than the one I've described.
